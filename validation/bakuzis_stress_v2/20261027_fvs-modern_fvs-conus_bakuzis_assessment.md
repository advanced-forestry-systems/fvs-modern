This memo reviews the long-run realism (Bakuzis) evidence behind the two FVS manuscripts as they stand in the connected folders (`FVS_Modern`, `FVS_CONUS`, both dated 4 August 2026) and in the three GitHub repositories (`fvs_remodeling` at 9ead428, `fvs-conus-pipeline` at ff12f81, `fvs-calibration` at 5cfa883, plus `fvs-modern` at 895f5aa, which is where the latest Bakuzis code and data live). ## 1. What exists today

There are four separate Bakuzis implementations across the repositories, written at different times with different definitions of the same named relations, and none of them is the one that produced Paper 2 Table 6.

The fvs-calibration synthetic gate (`conus_integration_pipeline/`, 4 July 2026) projects pure even-aged stands (3 species by 4 site index classes, 720 TPA start, 22 ten-year cycles) through the native FVS variants with and without the Greg diameter-growth hook. It is the most defensible design of the four because the site axis is real and the stands are dense enough to self-thin. Its Reineke test uses a fixed acceptance window of -1.3 to -1.8 on an age-screened limb and reports slopes near -2.0 for every native and hook arm as "borderline" in the memo and FLAG in the CSV. Its own caveat notes the IE variant does not respond to SITECODE, so site ordering there is a trivial pass.

The fvs-modern engine verdict (`validation/bakuzis_engine_verdict/`, promoted 7 September 2026) runs 36 synthetic scenarios (4 species groups by 3 sites by 3 densities, 20 five-year cycles) through the compiled NE library with GOMPIT mortality via fvs2py and scores five relations in `bakuzis_final_verdict.R`. Two of the five verdicts are hardcoded, since site ordering is assigned `FLAG-INCONCLUSIVE` with a comment that SETSITE did not vary SITEAR, and yield-density is assigned `PASS` with no test behind it. Eichhorn is defined as top-height invariance to initial density, which is not Eichhorn's rule (volume as a function of dominant height independent of site). The data table this verdict was run on is committed, and Section 3 below shows what it contains.

The fvs-modern uncertainty pipeline (`calibration/python/bakuzis_uncertainty_*.py`, the basis of Paper 1 Section 3.3, 4.6 and Figures 8 to 10) defines Eichhorn's rule as basal area non-decreasing across site classes, which again is not Eichhorn, and reports the Sukachev effect as 0.0 for the NE variant on all three configurations in `bakuzis_laws_compliance.csv` without that number reaching the manuscript text I read. The mortality size law is NA by design. The committed posterior-median ensemble is examined in Section 4.

Paper 2 Table 6 (b1 and b2 diameter growth crossed with GOMPIT and native mortality, with BA and AGB inversion counts and a fitted frontier slope) comes from the CONUS projector v4 run on real FIA stands. The projector itself is versioned (`validation/conus_projector/conus_eq_projector_v4.R`) and emits the metrics table the scorer needs, but the scorer that produced the PASS and FLAG cells, the inversion counts and the slopes is not in any of the four repositories. The Paper 2 STATUS note already places that section on HOLD pending array 13309592. Until the scorer is versioned, Table 6 is not reproducible from the repositories, and the numbers in it (for example the b1_native slope of -0.886) cannot be traced to code.

Two cross-cutting defects apply to all of the above. None of the scorers reports how many trajectories each relation was evaluated on, so a clean cell can sit on an axis that was never tested, which is the defect recorded against the bundled skill script on 7 September 2026. And none carries a magnitude check against the observed distribution, so a projection can pass every named relation while ending far above any basal area ever measured.

## 2. The v2 scorer

`bakuzis_stress_v2.R` replaces the four scorers with one. It reads any of the versioned trajectory sources through an adapter (`--source=engine|projector|gate|uncert|long`), converts to metric, and scores nine relations per trajectory and per arm by group, printing evaluated, not evaluated, not applicable and flagged counts for every relation. No verdict is hardcoded. The tests are:

T0 initial-step shock, flagging a stand that loses more than 25 percent of stems or basal area in its first projection step. T1 monotone development of dominant height and QMD, with QMD declared not diagnostic when stems are being added. T2 site ordering of height and volume within density level at every age, with an explicit inert-axis detection that reports NOT EVALUATED when site levels produce identical trajectories. T3 the Reineke slope, step-aware as in the September defect fix (the QMD screen is a rate in cm per year multiplied by each trajectory's own step) and now additionally gated on relative density, so that when SDImax is supplied only points at SDI/SDImax at or above 0.55 enter the fit, and a trajectory that never reaches the zone is NOT APPLICABLE with its peak relative density printed, rather than FLAG or PASS. T3b a pooled frontier slope by quantile regression (tau 0.95) on the same limb. T4 peak SDI against SDImax and maximum basal area against an observed 99.9th percentile when one is supplied. T5 Eichhorn as the coefficient of variation of volume across sites at matched dominant height. T6 Sukachev, per-tree volume falling with initial density within site. T7 yield-density, final basal area not falling with initial density by more than 10 percent. T8 a pinned-plateau detector, which flags when trajectories that started with widely different basal area finish within 3 percent of one another. T9 stand-level inversions, counting steps where basal area or volume falls by more than 10 percent.

The script writes the metric long table, a per-trajectory scorecard, a per-arm summary, the Reineke rows on their own, a Leary-form matrix figure per arm and group, and a log-log stems-against-QMD figure per arm with the Reineke slope drawn through the maximum SDI reached. Projection step, `qmd_rate`, the RD gate and the form factor are printed in the header.

## 3. Results: the fvs-modern engine verdict data (NE library, GOMPIT mortality, 36 scenarios, 5 year step)

Source: `validation/bakuzis_engine_verdict/engine_bakuzis_out/engine_bakuzis_100yr.csv`. SDImax by group taken from the verdict script's own comment values (506, 560, 529, 560 trees per acre at 10 in, converted to per hectare). Step 5 yr, `qmd_rate` 0.10 cm per year, RD gate 0.55.

The headline is that this dataset does not support any realism claim for the GOMPIT-equipped NE engine, and the committed verdict script could not see why.

Every Spruce-Fir scenario collapses in the first cycle. Stems fall by 89 to 98 percent and basal area by 81 to 94 percent between year 0 and year 5, from 151 to 396 TPA down to 5 to 16 TPA, and the stands then sit at 3 to 5 TPA and 12 to 13 ft² ac⁻¹ for the remaining 95 years. Nine of nine T0 shocks flag, nine of nine basal area inversions flag, and QMD is non-monotone in eight of nine because the survivors are a handful of trees. Six of nine Pine scenarios lose 38 to 69 percent of stems in the first cycle, and all Pine scenarios converge on a basal area of 129 to 130 ft² ac⁻¹ regardless of start, which the plateau detector flags (final basal area CV 1.7 percent across nine trajectories that started with CV 42 percent). Oak-Pine does the opposite, as the three high-density scenarios run to peak SDI 1.6 times the engine SDImax and finish at 579 to 649 ft² ac⁻¹, with trajectory Reineke slopes of -0.33 to -0.59 and a pooled frontier slope of -0.41 against -1.605, which is a mortality model that does not respond to density. Northern Hardwood is the only group that behaves, with three high-density trajectories carry slopes of -1.34 to -1.56, the pooled frontier is -1.60, Eichhorn CV is 7 percent and the SDI ratio peaks at 0.90.

Site ordering flags in all four groups (height inversions in 1 to 18 of 63 density by age cells, volume inversions in 12 to 63 of 63). This is consistent with the verdict script's note that SETSITE did not reach the engine; the only site signal in these runs is the initial height the stand generator wrote from site index, so ordering decays with age. The site axis of this harness is not usable until the keyword is fixed, and the correct report for it is NOT EVALUATED, which is what the verdict script's `FLAG-INCONCLUSIVE` was trying to say.

The Reineke axis was evaluated on 14 of 36 trajectories (the rest never reach the self-thinning zone after the first-cycle collapse), 11 of which flag. The committed verdict reported "0/36 scenarios decline" on top height, which is true and irrelevant, and did not test the two relations that fail.

What this points to in the model. The first-cycle collapse in Spruce-Fir and Pine and the runaway in Oak-Pine are the same submodel seen from both ends, since the GOMPIT survival transform as compiled, with the Greg coefficient set, is wrong in magnitude for softwood-dominated stands and insensitive to density in oak. The verdict memo for the same date records an A/B between the GOMPIT transform and an exponential hazard transform (`engine_ab.py`), which is the right experiment; the Bakuzis data say the GOMPIT arm should not have been reported as passing. The near-term Paper 2 scorecard, where GOMPIT retains 88.5 percent of stems against 87.3 percent observed over one remeasurement interval, does not contradict this, as a transform can be right on average over a 5 year FIA interval on mixed stands and still annihilate a pure spruce-fir cohort in a single step, which is why both gates are needed.

## 4. Results: the fvs-modern FIA-stand posterior-median ensemble (NE and ACD, default and calibrated MAP)

Source: `calibration/output/bakuzis/bakuzis_uncertainty_summary_long.csv`, posterior medians per scenario and year, configurations `default` and `calibrated_map`, 27 NE and 30 ACD populated cells. This is the dataset behind Paper 1 Figures 8 to 10.

The density axis of the design did not reach the engine. Cells defined as Low, Medium and High initial basal area (60, 120 and 180 ft² ac⁻¹ in the manuscript's Section 3.3) start at 1.1 to 2.4 m² ha⁻¹ (5 to 11 ft² ac⁻¹) with 94 to 272 stems per hectare, and the High cells are not denser than the Low cells. Peak SDI/SDImax over 100 years is 0.46 in the densest ACD cell and 0.47 in NE, so no scenario enters the self-thinning zone and the Reineke axis is NOT APPLICABLE on all 114 trajectories. Sukachev and yield-density flag in every arm because the density labels carry no density. Eichhorn flags in 14 of 16 arm by group cells with volume CV across sites of 16 to 49 percent at matched height.

The most likely cause, which I have not verified, is that the FIA tree records were written to TREEINIT without their per-acre expansion factors, or with a plot-level rather than subplot-level factor, so the engine sees a handful of trees per acre. It should be checked against the treeinit writer in `calibration/python/inventory_keyfile.py` before anything else is done with this ensemble. The default and calibrated MAP arms are also identical to every printed digit in all ACD cells and nearly so in NE, which is consistent with Paper 1's "nearly co-located" finding but is also what one would expect if parameter differences cannot express themselves on near-empty stands. Figures 8 to 10 and the Section 4.6 conclusion that "the recalibration's principal value is uncertainty quantification rather than a shift of long horizon central tendencies" rest on this ensemble and should be held until the seeding is confirmed.

## 7. Rerun on the main-branch engine built on firebreather (both survival arms, 36 scenarios, 5 year step)

The engine verdict data in Section 3 came from a Cardinal worktree build (`wt-engine`, branch `reconcile/three-arm-onto-main`, `lib_gompit`). To test whether main itself produces the collapse, FVSne was rebuilt on firebreather from `fvs-modern` at 895f5aa with `deployment/scripts/build_fvs_libraries.sh` (one link flag added, `-Wl,--no-as-needed -ldl`, because fvs2py looks `dlclose` up on the FVS handle and glibc 2.31 keeps it in libdl), and the 36 scenarios were run with `engine_bakuzis_v2.py` under GOMPIT mortality (`FVS_GOMPIT=1`, `config/greg_mortality_coefficients.csv`) and under native NE mortality, on identical stands. The harness writes SITECODE in place of the v1 SETSITE line, and every scenario's `.out` now reports the designed site index (45, 60 or 75 ft), so the site axis is live for the first time; the seeding gate passed 36 of 36 in both arms (year-0 stems within 1 percent and basal area within 2 percent of the design).

On this build neither arm collapses. There are no first-step shocks (worst stem loss 15 percent, in a Northern Hardwood high-density cell under native mortality), dominant height and QMD are monotone in 36 of 36, site ordering of height and volume holds in every density by age cell for all four groups, Eichhorn passes in all four groups under both arms, peak SDI stays below the engine SDImax in all 36 (relative density at year 100 reaches 0.65 under GOMPIT and 0.76 under native in Spruce-Fir), maximum basal area is 55 m² ha⁻¹ under GOMPIT and 62 m² ha⁻¹ under native, and no basal area or volume inversion occurs. The 70.6 m² ha⁻¹ threshold used in that run is the CONUS-wide observed 99th percentile from `score_arm.R`; against the Northeast fitting data alone (Section 11: p99 51.3, p99.9 62.7 m² ha⁻¹) both arms' densest synthetic stands sit above the 99th percentile and the native arm touches the 99.9th, so on the engine too the eastern basal area envelope is the thing to watch. The Reineke axis was evaluated on 20 of 36 trajectories under GOMPIT (3 flagged, Pine and Spruce-Fir, medians by group -2.06, -1.79, -1.57, -1.48) and 23 of 36 under native (7 flagged, medians -2.04, -1.75, -1.38, -1.01 for Northern Hardwood, Oak-Pine, Pine, Spruce-Fir); the remaining trajectories never reach the 0.55 relative-density gate and are reported not applicable. The pooled frontier slope passes in every group that reaches the zone. The one relation that flags in every group under both arms is Sukachev, since per-tree volume at year 100 does not fall with initial density; the stands have converged on a common carrying capacity by then, and the test should be read at a mid-rotation age, which is a scorer refinement rather than a model fault.

Two conclusions follow. The spruce-fir and pine first-cycle collapse and the oak-pine runaway in the committed verdict data are properties of the Cardinal worktree build and its library pair rather than of main; the committed `engine_bakuzis_100yr.csv` should be withdrawn from the validation folder or relabeled with its build. And on main the GOMPIT arm is slightly cleaner than native on the self-thinning axis (3 flags against 7, Spruce-Fir slope -1.48 against -1.01), which is the direction Paper 2 claims, now with evidence that reports what it evaluated.

## 8. The Table 6 scorer, recovered from Cardinal scratch (corrected late October 27)

The scorer that produced Paper 2 Table 6 is `/fs/scratch/PUOM0008/crsfaaron/fvs_stress/conus_eq_proj/bakuzis_assess.R` with `bakuzis_assess_eichhorn.R` and `build_bakuzis_traj.R`, last written October 9, 2026 and never committed. Its `bakuzis_passflag.csv` carries Table 6 exactly (frontier slopes -1.461, -0.886, -1.391, -1.381; BA inversions 1, 0, 0, 0; AGB inversions 3, 11, 0, 0) and two columns Table 6 does not report: the 99th percentile of terminal-year per-stand SDI, computed per acre as TPA (QMD_in / 10)^1.605, is 546 and 581 for the GOMPIT arms and 1,460 and 1,085 for the native arms. Converted to per hectare (times 2.471) those are 1,349, 1,436, 3,608 and 2,681 trees ha⁻¹. The FIA remeasurement pairs the arms were seeded from carry an observed 99.9th percentile of SDI between 1,130 (CS) and 2,230 (PN) trees ha⁻¹ by variant, 1,498 for NE, so the native-mortality arms run to two to three times the observed ceiling and the GOMPIT arms sit at or just above it. That is the magnitude result Table 6 should have reported, and it supports the direction of the paper's mortality claim while contradicting the reading that b2_native is as clean as b2_gompit.

The Eichhorn PASS in Table 6 (`eichhorn_scores.csv`) is a regression of aboveground biomass on dominant height with and without site class, and the pass criterion is that site class reduces RMSE by at most 1.2 percent. The R² of biomass on dominant height alone is 0.30 (b1_native), 0.42 (b2_native), 0.50 (b2_gompit) and 0.57 (b1_gompit), so the finding is that site adds nothing to a weak relation, not that biomass collapses onto height, which is how the manuscript words it.

A second scorer, `/fs/scratch/PUOM0008/crsfaaron/bakuzis/score_arm.R` (also October 9, also unversioned), scores each arm against an observed Bakuzis matrix built from the same pairs and is the source of the morning claim, carried into PR 114's commit message, that every CONUS arm exceeds the observed SDI ceiling by 1.7 to 2.6 times. That claim is a unit error inside `score_arm.R`: it computes arm SDI per hectare from TPH but takes the observed ceiling from the pairs file's SDI column, which is per acre (the file's TPA1 is per acre while BA1 and QMD1 are metric; its NE SDI1 median of 241 reproduces as 533 trees ac⁻¹ times (15.55 cm / 25.4)^1.605). On a common per-hectare basis the observed ceiling it used is about 2,310 trees ha⁻¹, against which the arm 99th percentiles of 1,594 (gompit), 2,052 (b2) and 2,428 (b1) are 0.7, 0.9 and 1.05 times. Its realism index inherits the same mismatch through the relative-density cells and should not be quoted. The third family, `bakuzis_20260804/` and `bakuzis_balfix_20260805/` (nine relations on 440 FIA plots over 150 years, five near-term arms), flags Reineke on fvs_base and fvs_regional (mean slopes -1.01 and -1.20, five of eight site by density groups outside the window) and passes organon (-1.86), with the PTBALT repair changing nothing for the native arms.

The scripts, READMEs, run files and summary tables of all three families (no plot lists, no trajectories) are committed under `validation/bakuzis_stress_v2/cardinal_scorers/` and `table6_scorer/`, so Table 6 is now traceable to code.

## 11. Projector v4 arms rescored on Cardinal with the magnitude test live

The four CONUS projector arms behind Table 6 (`out_conus_eq` for b1 and b2 native, `out_conus_eq_b1_gompit`, `out_conus_eq_b2_gompit`; 17 to 19 variant files each, 5 year step, real FIA stands) were rescored with `bakuzis_stress_v2.R` on 1,500 randomly drawn stands per variant per arm (seed 20261027; 24,097 to 27,097 trajectories per arm). The observed thresholds came from the fitting data itself, `conus_remeasurement_pairs_metric_cond_v2_cspiv6.rds`, deduplicated to plots: BA 99th and 99.9th percentiles and SDI 99.9th percentile by variant, with the file's SDI1 and TPA1 converted from per acre to per hectare (NE: BA p99 51.3 and p99.9 62.7 m² ha⁻¹, SDI p99.9 1,498 trees ha⁻¹; SN 48.3, 61.8, 1,443; PN 104.6, 105.6, 2,230 on 38 plots). The metrics files carry no dominant height, so the height, site and volume relations report NOT EVALUATED for all four arms; what is live is the first-step shock, QMD monotonicity, the relative-density-gated Reineke slope, the pooled frontier, SDI and basal area against the observed percentiles, the plateau detector and basal area inversions.

The magnitude test separates the mortality arms cleanly. Under native mortality 35 percent (b1) and 28 percent (b2) of stands finish above the observed 99.9th percentile of basal area for their variant, and 22 and 17 percent exceed the observed SDI ceiling; under GOMPIT the figures are 12 and 13 percent above the basal area p99.9 and 5 and 4 percent above the SDI ceiling. The failure is concentrated in the eastern variants. In the b1_native arm 1,444 of 1,500 NE stands and 1,472 of 1,500 SN stands exceed the basal area p99.9, the NE median peak SDI is 1.23 times the ceiling (SN 1.50, CS 1.33, LS 1.18), and the trajectory Reineke slopes in NE, LS, CS and SN are -0.14, -0.14, -0.37 and +0.12, so stems are not being removed as the stands densify; the western variants in the same arm sit at -1.6 to -1.7 with few exceedances. Under b2_gompit the NE median peak SDI is 0.78 (188 of 1,500 over), 835 of 1,500 NE stands still exceed the basal area p99.9, and the trajectory slopes run -1.3 to -1.9 across variants (NE -1.57). The pooled upper-edge frontier passes in nearly every variant under every arm (NE -1.91 b1_native, -1.97 b2_gompit), because the projector's SDImax cap (its FIX 3) shapes the densest stands even where typical stands do not self-thin, which is why the frontier alone was never a sufficient test. The GOMPIT arms carry more single-step basal area drops above 10 percent (50 percent of trajectories against 27 to 31 percent under native), which reads as mortality arriving in pulses on a 5 year step and is worth a look at the cycle-level survival. The Reineke axis was applicable on 9,297 to 14,424 trajectories per arm and not applicable on 11,887 to 15,481 that never reach a relative density of 0.55, which is the expected count for a sample of real inventory plots.

Read against Table 6, the picture is the same in direction and larger in degree. b1_native is the worst arm on every live relation; b2_gompit is the best; but no arm is clean on magnitude in the Northeast, and the manuscript's "zero inversions" cells for the b2 arms do not survive a sample of 1,500 stands per variant. Section 3.4 should report the exceedance fractions by variant beside the slopes.

## 12. Regenerating the Paper 1 ensemble: the double expansion, and what remains

The Northeast ensemble runs through the FVS_StandInit database path, not the inventory keyfile, so the August 27 keyfile fix never applied to it. The defect is in `fia_stand_generator.py`, which writes `inv_plot_size: 6.0` while `tree_count` is `TPA_UNADJ`, already per acre; FVS multiplies each record by the inverse plot size, so every tree entered at six times its expansion. A six-cell NE smoke run on the main-branch library built in a clean worktree seeded at 450 to 28,788 TPA and 4 to 655 ft² ac⁻¹ against labels of 60 to 180. With `inv_plot_size` set to 1.0 the same cells seed at 0 to 4,798 TPA and 0 to 109 ft² ac⁻¹, so the six-fold inflation is gone, but the stands still do not reproduce the condition basal area they were selected on (ratios 0.02 to 1.5 of the cell label; one replicate has no trees). The remaining loss is upstream of the keyfile, in the trees `load_fia_trees()` attaches to a sampled condition, and it needs a look at the PLT_CN join and the condition filter before the 100-draw array is worth running. The patch, the two smoke runs and the seed checks are in PR 115; the July 10 ensemble, which carried a different seeding fault (year-0 stems in multiples of 36 TPA and nine empty cells), should be withdrawn from `calibration/output/bakuzis/` once its replacement exists.
